Utility Functions and Insurance Decisions

The same hurricane, the same wealth numbers — drag the two dots on each chart (wealth with no disaster / with disaster), or adjust the probability, and watch how three different utility function shapes lead to three completely different insurance decisions.

A Florida homeowner faces the same hurricane risk: wealth of $400k with no hurricane, wealth of $200k if the hurricane hits, with a 5% probability of the hurricane occurring. All three homeowners face the identical risk — but different utility function shapes lead them to different decisions.
Drag the dots directly on the charts to change either state's wealth value (in $10k units)
The comparison between E[U(W)] and U(E[W]) follows from Jensen's inequality: when the utility function is concave (U''<0), E[U(W)] < U(E[W]) — meaning a certain sum of money is "worth more" than a fluctuating one of the same average value. This is the mathematical basis for why insurance exists. Risk premium RP = E(W) − CE, where CE (certainty equivalent) is the inverse utility function evaluated at E[U(W)].
